# 2ESO · LS2 · Achievement indicators (NA · AS · AN · AE)

**25 Oct 2026.** Step 5 of Roger's method (*Worked example: from competence to LS criterion*): for each LS assessment criterion of *Free Time, Live!*, four indicators that say how well the objective has been achieved. Steps 1–4 are in the [programme](../programme.md#ls2--2eso--free-time-live). Written by Claude under Roger's standing permission; the same texts are in `shared/js/el-grading.js` (`EL_GRADING["2eso-free-time-live"].ind`, the dashboard shows them on hover) and this table is generated from them: change them there and regenerate.

- **How the levels differ:** the help the student needs (model, template, checklist, subtitles or a second viewing), the complexity of what they do, and its quality.
- **How many times (Roger, 10 Nov 2026).** Each level keeps the quality and the help it described and adds how many times the student shows the indicator, with the same cuts as the mark: fewer than half = NA, half = AS, 7 in 10 = AN, 9 in 10 = AE (the mark out of 10 is the % of times the student showed what the indicators describe). For the tips, the number asked for by route does the same job. A level needs both the number and the quality. Each indicator starts with what the student can do; the numbers in brackets are the evidence.
- **One set for all routes.** Routes change the task and the help (Catalan guidance, templates, one given source or two sources, number of tips), not the indicators.
- **On the final product rubric:** NA = Level 1 (2.5) · AS = Level 2 (5) · AN = Level 3 (7.5) · AE = Level 4 (10). The descriptors in `2eso/free-time-live/final-rubric-data.js` follow these indicators, written for each route.
- **Where the evidence is:** see [assessment-criteria.md](assessment-criteria.md).

| **4.1** Reading log on two texts (O1) | Cannot yet pick out the key information in the texts or their purpose, even with the model (fewer than half of the facts right; sentences copied). | Picks out the basic information in two texts (what people are doing, where, when, what they usually do) with the model's help and identifies the purpose of one text (at least half of the facts right). | Picks out the key information in two texts in their own words and identifies each text's purpose with a word that shows it (7 in 10 facts right). | Understands the texts on their own, infers new words from context and compares how the two texts use language (9 in 10 facts right). |
| **4.2** Rating two sources (O2) | Cannot yet judge whether a page is reliable: gives no reasons or trusts an unreliable page (fewer than half of the reasons right). | Judges a page's reliability with criteria from the checklist and chooses one (at least half of the reasons right). | Judges the pages' reliability with relevant criteria (author, date, purpose, type of website) and chooses the better one (7 in 10 reasons right). | Judges the pages accurately on their own, considering the purpose (selling, getting views, informing), and justifies the choice (9 in 10 reasons right). |
| **2.2** Listening chart on two vlogs (O1) | Cannot yet identify what the vloggers are doing, even with subtitles (fewer than half of the boxes right). | Identifies what each vlogger is doing now or usually does, plus a detail, with subtitles or a second viewing (at least half of the boxes). | Identifies what each vlogger is doing now, what they usually do and details (place, clothes, weather), using the images and key words (7 in 10 boxes). | Understands the vlogs accurately, says which words or images helped and compares the two vloggers (9 in 10 boxes). |
| **3.2** The vlog: plan, record, revise (O3) | Cannot yet produce a vlog the audience can follow (does not record it, or reads more than half of the text). | Records the vlog with guidance, covers the basic content and contrasts now and usually simply (reads up to half; at least half of the sentences without errors). | Plans, records and revises the vlog; contrasts now (Present Continuous) and usually (Present Simple) clearly and improves one part (reads at most 2 sentences; 7 in 10 sentences without errors). | Presents a fluent, well-structured vlog with a natural contrast and improves it on their own (does not read; 9 in 10 sentences without errors; 1.5–2 minutes). |
| **5.1** Vlog script and slide text (O4) | Cannot yet write an understandable script with the parts of the model (fewer than half of the verbs right). | Writes the script with the parts of the model, following the template, and gets the message across (at least half of the verbs right). | Writes a complete script adapted from the model, with time expressions, like / love / hate + -ing and tips with imperatives (7 in 10 verbs right). | Writes a clear, well-linked script beyond the model and corrects their own errors (9 in 10 forms right; Plus: a temporary situation and an opinion). |
| **6.2** Fact file and credits (O2) | Cannot yet organise information from sources or credit them (fewer than half of the facts asked for; no credits). | Organises basic information from a source in a fact file, with help, and credits some sources and photos (half of the facts; at least half of the credits). | Organises the information in a fact file in key words and credits sources and photos correctly (3 in 4 facts; 7 in 10 credits complete). | Rewords the information from sources they compared and credits everything on their own (all the facts and all the credits). |
| **CD2 2.2** Recording and editing (O3) | Cannot yet edit the video: mistakes are left and the photos do not match the script (fewer than half of the photos in place). | Records and edits with guidance and fixes some of the mistakes (at least half of the photos with their part of the script). | Records and edits the vlog: cuts mistakes or pauses and matches the photos to the script (at least 1 cut; 7 in 10 photos in place). | Records and edits the vlog on their own, with a good rhythm and no mistakes left (no long pauses; 9 in 10 photos in place). |
| **CD4 4.5** Screen tips (O4) | Cannot yet suggest tips to avoid addictive screen use (fewer than half of the tips asked for). | Suggests general tips about screen use, close to the model (at least half of the tips asked for). | Suggests concrete tips that prevent addictive habits and chooses the one they will apply (all the tips asked for, with imperatives). | Justifies each tip and reflects on how they apply it to their own screen use (all the tips, each with a reason). |
| **process** Process evidence (steps 0–5) | Cannot yet document their process (fewer than 3 of the 6 steps handed in). | Documents part of the process (3 or 4 of the 6 steps handed in). | Documents the process step by step (5 of the 6 steps, or all 6 with one late or very general). | Documents the whole process specifically and assesses themselves honestly (all 6 steps on time and in their own words). |
