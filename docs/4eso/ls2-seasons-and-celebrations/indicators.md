# 4ESO · LS2 · Achievement indicators (NA · AS · AN · AE)

**24 Oct 2026.** Step 5 of Roger's method (*Worked example: from competence to LS criterion*): for each LS assessment criterion, four indicators that say how well the objective has been achieved. Steps 1–4 (competence, Decret criterion, LS objective, LS criterion) are in the [programme](../programme.md#ls2--4eso--seasons--celebrations). Written by Claude from the revised programme; live with PR #54 (merged 24 Oct 2026 at Roger's go-ahead). How these marks add up to the LS2 grade is being planned separately.

- **How the levels differ:** the help the student needs (model, template, checklist, a second viewing), the complexity of what they do, and its quality.
- **How many times (Roger, 10 Nov 2026).** Each level keeps the quality and the help it described and adds how many times the student shows the indicator, with the same cuts as the mark: fewer than half = NA, half = AS, 7 in 10 = AN, 9 in 10 = AE (the mark out of 10 is the % of times the student showed what the indicators describe). Where there is nothing to count (1.1, 1.2), the number of ideas or decisions does the same job. A level needs both the number and the quality. Each indicator starts with what the student can do; the numbers in brackets are the evidence.
- **One set for all routes.** Routes change the task and the help (Catalan guidance, templates, two or three sources, part length), not the indicators.
- **On the final product rubric:** NA = Level 1 (2.5) · AS = Level 2 (5) · AN = Level 3 (7.5) · AE = Level 4 (10). The rubric descriptors in `4eso/seasons-and-celebrations/final-rubric-data.js` are these indicators, written for each route.
- **Where the evidence is:** see [assessment-criteria.md](assessment-criteria.md).

| LS criterion (task) | NA · No assoliment | AS · Assoliment satisfactori | AN · Assoliment notable | AE · Assoliment excel·lent |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **4.1** Reading log on two texts (O1) | Cannot yet pick out the key information in the texts or infer words from context, even with the model (fewer than 5 of the 10 facts right; sentences copied). | Picks out the basic information in two texts (what, when, where, customs) with the model's help and infers a word from context (5 or 6 of the 10 facts; 1 of 2 words). | Picks out the key information in two texts in their own words, tells main customs from minor details and infers words from context (7 or 8 of the 10 facts; 2 of 2 words). | Understands the texts accurately on their own, makes inferences (why a custom matters) and explains how the context gives each word's meaning (9 or 10 of the 10 facts). |
| **4.2** Rating sources (O2) | Cannot yet judge whether a source is reliable: gives no reasons or trusts an unreliable source (fewer than half of the reasons right). | Judges a source's reliability with criteria from the checklist (author, date) and names the one they trust most (at least half of the reasons right). | Judges the sources' reliability with relevant criteria (author, date, purpose, channel) and justifies trusting one most (7 in 10 reasons right). | Judges sources critically on their own, considering purpose and channel, and sees the limits of a good source (9 in 10 reasons right). |
| **2.2** Listening chart on two videos (O1) | Cannot yet identify what the speakers value; only notes facts (dates, food), even with subtitles (fewer than 3 of the 6 boxes). | Identifies one value of each speaker and a word that shows it, with subtitles or a second viewing (3 or 4 of the 6 boxes). | Interprets in their own words what each speaker values and supports it with a phrase from the video and the tone or images (5 of the 6 boxes). | Interprets and compares what the two speakers value, with precise evidence from words, tone and images (6 of the 6 boxes and the comparison). |
| **3.2** Group pitch (O3) | Cannot yet plan and deliver their part so the audience can follow it (does not record it, or reads more than half of the text). | Plans and records their part with the basic content, from a script revised with help (reads up to half of the text; at least half of the sentences without errors). | Plans, rehearses and revises their part and presents it clearly and completely (reads at most 2 sentences; 7 in 10 sentences without errors in the structures worked on). | Presents their part fluently, adapted to an audience that does not know the celebration and linked to the next speaker (does not read; 9 in 10 sentences without errors). |
| **5.1** Formal email (O5) | Cannot yet write an understandable formal email: the purpose or the request is missing (fewer than half of the future forms right). | Writes an email with purpose, plan and request following the template and gets the message across (at least half of the future forms right). | Writes a well-organised formal email (purpose, plan, request, closing) in formal register, with future forms and a relative clause (7 in 10 future forms right). | Writes a well-linked, persuasive formal email beyond the model and corrects their own errors (9 in 10 forms right; Plus: answers a concern). |
| **1.1** Meaning and place at school (O3) | Cannot yet explain what the celebration means for the people who celebrate it, or presents it in a judging way (no correct idea). | Explains simply what the celebration means and why it can be at school, close to the model (1 idea and 1 reason). | Explains what the celebration means for the people who celebrate it and argues for its place at school with respect for diversity (2 ideas and 2 reasons). | Values the celebration with nuance (not everyone celebrates it the same way, or it has changed) and argues by linking diversity to rights or the community (3 or more ideas). |
| **1.2** Local link and inclusion (O4) | Cannot yet build links between the celebration and local culture or propose inclusive decisions, or the plan includes a stereotype (no link or decision). | Builds a simple link with a local celebration and proposes an inclusive decision, close to the model (1 link and 1 decision; Base: one thing in common). | Compares the two celebrations clearly and fairly and justifies an inclusive decision (at least 1 similarity, 1 difference and 1 decision with a reason). | Builds a real link between the two cultures and foresees who could feel left out with several justified decisions (2 or more decisions). |
| **6.1** Research sheet (O2) | Cannot yet find and organise information from the required sources: copies notes and gives no references (fewer sources than required, or fewer than half of the notes in their own words). | Finds the key information in the required sources, with help (at least half of the notes in their own words; references incomplete). | Finds, selects and compares information from several sources, rewords it and references it correctly (7 in 10 notes in their own words; all references; 1 point where they agree or differ). | Finds and compares varied sources on their own and resolves a disagreement by choosing the more reliable one (9 in 10 notes in their own words and organised; full references). |
| **CC1 1.3** Welcome actions in the email (O5) | No concrete action, or only a vague one ("everybody can come"). | One concrete action, or two very general ones close to the model. | Two concrete, realistic actions for students and families (explaining the customs, inviting families, an alternative for those who do not celebrate it). | Two or more concrete actions with reasons, thinking of different people (families, students who do not celebrate it, newcomers). |
| **CC3 3.2** Stereotype in the pitch (O4) | No stereotype named, or the pitch repeats one (images, words, costumes). | Names one stereotype in a simple sentence with help ("Some people think…") and says the plan avoids it, without saying why it is unfair. | Names a stereotype, explains why it is unfair, and points to a concrete choice in the plan that avoids it. | Explains why it is unfair with an example or evidence from the research, and shows several choices in the plan that challenge it. |
