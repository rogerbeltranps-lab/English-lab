# 4ESO · LS2 · Achievement indicators (NA · AS · AN · AE)

**24 Oct 2026.** Step 5 of Roger's method (*Worked example: from competence to LS criterion*): for each LS assessment criterion, four indicators that say how well the objective has been achieved. Steps 1–4 (competence, Decret criterion, LS objective, LS criterion) are in the [programme](../programme.md#ls2--4eso--seasons--celebrations). Written by Claude from the revised programme; **waiting for Roger's approval**.

- **How the levels differ:** the help the student needs (model, template, checklist, a second viewing), the complexity of what they do, and its quality.
- **One set for all routes.** Routes change the task and the help (Catalan guidance, templates, two or three sources, part length), not the indicators.
- **On the final product rubric:** NA = Level 1 (2.5) · AS = Level 2 (5) · AN = Level 3 (7.5) · AE = Level 4 (10). The rubric descriptors in `4eso/seasons-and-celebrations/final-rubric-data.js` are these indicators, written for each route.
- **Where the evidence is:** see [assessment-criteria.md](assessment-criteria.md).

| LS criterion (task) | NA · No assoliment | AS · Assoliment satisfactori | AN · Assoliment notable | AE · Assoliment excel·lent |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **4.1** Reading log on two texts (O1) | The log is unfinished or mostly wrong, even with the model: facts missing for one text, whole sentences copied, no word inferred. | Both texts logged with what, when, where and two customs, close to the model and often copied; one word inferred correctly. | Both texts logged accurately in short notes of their own, main customs chosen over minor details, two words inferred correctly. | Accurate, concise log without help; adds an idea that needs inference (why a custom matters) and explains how the context showed each word's meaning. |
| **4.2** Rating sources (O2) | Sources rated with no reasons, or an obviously unreliable source rated as reliable (a shop blog, an anonymous post), even with the checklist. | Each source rated with one or two reasons copied from the checklist (author, date); names the one they trust most. | Each source rated correctly with two relevant reasons (author, date, purpose, channel); explains why they trust one most. | Rates on their own with precise reasons, including purpose and channel (selling, entertaining); sees the limits of a good source and compares them. |
| **2.2** Listening chart on two videos (O1) | Chart missing, or it lists only facts (dates, food) and no values, even after a second viewing with subtitles. | Names one thing each speaker values (Insecure: from a list) and copies one word or phrase that shows it, with subtitles or a second viewing. | Explains in their own words what each speaker values, with a phrase from the video and a note on the tone or the images. | Explains the values with precise evidence from words, tone and images, and compares the two speakers: what they share and how they differ. |
| **3.2** Group pitch (O3) | Does not prepare or record their part, or reads an unrevised text the audience cannot follow; does not take part in planning. | Records their part with the group and covers the basic content (what, when, where, customs) from a script revised with help; reads parts of it. | Plans and rehearses with the group; records a clear, complete part from a script they revised after the rehearsal (explains an unknown word, adds an example); only glances at the notes. | Leads planning or rehearsals; a fluent, well-structured part adapted to an audience that does not know the celebration, with links to the next speaker; uses feedback on their own. |
| **5.1** Formal email (O5) | Unfinished or not understandable; no clear purpose or request, or informal, even with the template. | Purpose, plan and request present, close to the template or model; future forms mostly correct; errors that do not block meaning; mostly formal. | Clear paragraphs (purpose, plan, request, closing), formal register, future forms and a relative clause used correctly; adapts the model. | Clear, well-linked and persuasive beyond the model (varied linkers, polite requests; Daring: a concern answered), few errors, corrected on their own. |
| **1.1** Meaning and place at school (O3) | Does not say what the celebration means for the people who celebrate it, or presents it in a judging way. | Says what it means in one simple sentence ("For many Hindus, Diwali means…") and gives one reason for it at school, close to the model. | Explains what it means for the people who celebrate it and argues for its place at school with respect for cultural diversity. | Explains the meaning with nuance (not everyone celebrates it the same way, or it has changed) and argues convincingly, linking diversity to rights or to the school community. |
| **1.2** Local link and inclusion (O4) | No link with a local celebration or no inclusive decision; or the plan includes a stereotype or a caricature costume. | One simple link (Insecure: one thing in common; Safe and Daring: one similarity and one difference) and one inclusive decision, close to the model. | A clear, fair comparison and an inclusive decision with a reason. | A comparison that builds a real link between the two cultures, and several justified decisions that foresee who could feel left out. |
| **6.1** Research sheet (O2) | Fewer sources than required, copied notes, no references, or wrong information. | Required sources with key information from each, with help; references partly complete; agreement or difference missing or vague. | Required sources, key information in their own words, full references, and one point where the sources agree or differ. | Varied, well-chosen sources, organised notes, an accurate comparison, and a disagreement resolved by choosing the more reliable source. |
| **CC1 1.3** Welcome actions in the email (O5) | No concrete action, or only a vague one ("everybody can come"). | One concrete action, or two very general ones close to the model. | Two concrete, realistic actions for students and families (explaining the customs, inviting families, an alternative for those who do not celebrate it). | Two or more concrete actions with reasons, thinking of different people (families, students who do not celebrate it, newcomers). |
| **CC3 3.2** Stereotype in the pitch (O4) | No stereotype named, or the pitch repeats one (images, words, costumes). | Names one stereotype in a simple sentence with help ("Some people think…") and says the plan avoids it, without saying why it is unfair. | Names a stereotype, explains why it is unfair, and points to a concrete choice in the plan that avoids it. | Explains why it is unfair with an example or evidence from the research, and shows several choices in the plan that challenge it. |
