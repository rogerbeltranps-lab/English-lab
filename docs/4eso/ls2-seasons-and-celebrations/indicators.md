# 4ESO · LS2 · Achievement indicators (NA · AS · AN · AE)

**24 Oct 2026.** Step 5 of Roger's method (*Worked example: from competence to LS criterion*): for each LS assessment criterion, four indicators that say how well the objective has been achieved. Steps 1–4 (competence, Decret criterion, LS objective, LS criterion) are in the [programme](../programme.md#ls2--4eso--seasons--celebrations). Written by Claude from the revised programme; live with PR #54 (merged 24 Oct 2026 at Roger's go-ahead). How these marks add up to the LS2 grade is being planned separately.

- **How the levels differ:** the help the student needs (model, template, checklist, a second viewing), the complexity of what they do, and its quality.
- **How many times (Roger, 10 Nov 2026).** Each level keeps the quality and the help it described and adds how many times the student shows the indicator, with the same cuts as the mark: fewer than half = NA, half = AS, 7 in 10 = AN, 9 in 10 = AE (the mark out of 10 is the % of times the student showed what the indicators describe). Where there is nothing to count (1.1, 1.2), the number of ideas or decisions does the same job. A level needs both the number and the quality.
- **One set for all routes.** Routes change the task and the help (Catalan guidance, templates, two or three sources, part length), not the indicators.
- **On the final product rubric:** NA = Level 1 (2.5) · AS = Level 2 (5) · AN = Level 3 (7.5) · AE = Level 4 (10). The rubric descriptors in `4eso/seasons-and-celebrations/final-rubric-data.js` are these indicators, written for each route.
- **Where the evidence is:** see [assessment-criteria.md](assessment-criteria.md).

| LS criterion (task) | NA · No assoliment | AS · Assoliment satisfactori | AN · Assoliment notable | AE · Assoliment excel·lent |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **4.1** Reading log on two texts (O1) | Fewer than 5 of the 10 facts right (what, when, where and two customs for each text), or whole sentences copied; no word inferred, even with the model. | 5 or 6 of the 10 facts right, close to the model and often copied; 1 of the 2 words inferred correctly. | 7 or 8 of the 10 facts right, in short notes of their own, main customs chosen over minor details; 2 of 2 words inferred correctly. | 9 or 10 of the 10 facts right, without help; adds an idea that needs inference (why a custom matters) and explains how the context showed each word's meaning. |
| **4.2** Rating sources (O2) | Fewer than half of the reasons right, or an obviously unreliable source (a shop blog, an anonymous post) rated as reliable, even with the checklist. | At least half of the reasons right, copied from the checklist (author, date); names the source they trust most. | 7 in 10 reasons right and relevant (author, date, purpose, channel); explains why they trust one source most. | 9 in 10 reasons right, found on their own, including purpose and channel (selling, entertaining); sees the limits of a good source. |
| **2.2** Listening chart on two videos (O1) | Fewer than 3 of the 6 boxes right (for each video: a value, a phrase that shows it, the tone or an image), or only facts (dates, food) and no values, even after a second viewing with subtitles. | 3 or 4 of the 6 boxes right: one thing each speaker values (Base: from a list) and one word or phrase that shows it, with subtitles or a second viewing. | 5 of the 6 boxes right, in their own words: what each speaker values, a phrase from the video and a note on the tone or the images. | 6 of the 6 boxes right, and compares the two speakers: what they share and how they differ. |
| **3.2** Group pitch (O3) | Does not record their part, or reads more than half of the text; the audience cannot follow the message. | Records their part with the basic content (what, when, where, customs) from a script revised with help; reads up to half of the text. | A clear, complete part, revised after the rehearsal; reads at most 2 whole sentences; 7 in 10 sentences without errors in the structures worked on. | Fluent and adapted to an audience that does not know the celebration, linked to the next speaker; does not read; 9 in 10 sentences without errors. |
| **5.1** Formal email (O5) | Unfinished or not understandable; no purpose or request; fewer than half of the future forms right. | Purpose, plan and request, close to the template; at least half of the future forms right; errors that do not block meaning. | Four clear parts (purpose, plan, request, closing), formal register; 7 in 10 future forms right and one relative clause used correctly. | Well linked and persuasive beyond the model (varied linkers; Plus: answers a concern); 9 in 10 forms right; corrects their own errors. |
| **1.1** Meaning and place at school (O3) | Does not say what the celebration means, or presents it in a judging way. | 1 correct idea about what it means for the people who celebrate it and 1 reason for having it at school, close to the model. | 2 ideas about its meaning and 2 reasons for having it at school, with respect for cultural diversity. | 3 or more ideas with nuance (not everyone celebrates it the same way, or it has changed) and arguments that link diversity to rights or to the school community. |
| **1.2** Local link and inclusion (O4) | No link with a local celebration and no inclusive decision, or the plan includes a stereotype. | 1 simple link (Base: one thing in common; Core and Plus: one similarity and one difference) and 1 inclusive decision, close to the model. | A clear, fair comparison (at least 1 similarity and 1 difference) and 1 inclusive decision with a reason. | A comparison that builds a real link between the two cultures, and 2 or more justified decisions that foresee who could feel left out. |
| **6.1** Research sheet (O2) | Fewer sources than required, or fewer than half of the notes in their own words; no references. | The required sources; at least half of the notes in their own words; references incomplete. | The required sources; 7 in 10 notes in their own words; all references complete; 1 point where the sources agree or differ. | Varied sources; 9 in 10 notes in their own words and organised; full references; a disagreement resolved by choosing the more reliable source. |
| **CC1 1.3** Welcome actions in the email (O5) | No concrete action, or only a vague one ("everybody can come"). | One concrete action, or two very general ones close to the model. | Two concrete, realistic actions for students and families (explaining the customs, inviting families, an alternative for those who do not celebrate it). | Two or more concrete actions with reasons, thinking of different people (families, students who do not celebrate it, newcomers). |
| **CC3 3.2** Stereotype in the pitch (O4) | No stereotype named, or the pitch repeats one (images, words, costumes). | Names one stereotype in a simple sentence with help ("Some people think…") and says the plan avoids it, without saying why it is unfair. | Names a stereotype, explains why it is unfair, and points to a concrete choice in the plan that avoids it. | Explains why it is unfair with an example or evidence from the research, and shows several choices in the plan that challenge it. |
