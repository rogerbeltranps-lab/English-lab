## 1. Groups

- **Same-route groups** (Roger, 24 Oct 2026): every group is Insecure, Safe or Daring, and the whole product follows that route's requirements and rubric.
- **The teacher forms the groups** inside each route, not friends' choice, so pairs that do not work well can be separated.
- Size: Insecure groups of 3; Safe and Daring groups of 3–4. If a route has too few students for a group, those students join the nearest route's group and keep their own route for their part and email.
- Formed in **week 2 (2–6 Nov)**, before Learning Path 3, and the group chooses its celebration then. Pieces 2, 4 and 5 in the Learning Paths are about the group's celebration, so the groups must exist before them even though the final product itself is made in term 2.
- Support for Insecure groups: slide template, sentence frames for every part, Catalan guidance on the page, and a short check-in with the teacher every class.

## 2. What each student hands in first: the preparation dossier

Before the group records the video, every student submits an **individual preparation dossier** on the Final Product page (one submission, then locked). It pushes everyone to do the Learning Path work, and it is the evidence of each student's own contribution.

| Piece | From | What the student pastes or completes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 · Description of a celebration | Learning Path 2 | Final version of the text |
| 2 · Our celebration | Learning Path 3 | Final version of the text |
| 3 · Research card | Learning Path 4 | Their copy of the group's card, with the two credit lines |
| 4 · Event plan | Learning Path 5 | Their version of the plan (route fields) |
| 5 · Email draft | Learning Path 6 | The draft before AI feedback |
| For pieces 1, 2 and 5 | — | One sentence: *What I changed after the feedback, and why* |

- The pieces saved in each Learning Path appear already filled in, so the student only reviews them; a missing piece must be written or pasted before submitting.
- The teacher sees the dossier on the dashboard (the texts are already synced; only a view is added).
- Deadline: the class before the recording (about 4 Dec). A student without a dossier records their part after handing it in.

## 3. What the group makes together

1. **Research card** (route fields as in Learning Path 4, piece 3), with two sources and credit lines.
2. **Event plan** for a school celebration day, at the route's level.
3. **Google Slides:** one or two slides per part, short text and images, credits on the last slide.
4. **Video:** every member presents their part, face and slides visible. Each part lasts what Learning Path 6 already says (Insecure 30–45 s, Safe 45–60 s, Daring 60–90 s).

## 4. Who presents what (each student owns one part)

| Route | Part 1 | Part 2 | Part 3 | Part 4 (groups of 4) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Insecure | What, when and where, and one food or symbol | Three customs | Our day: three activities, each with a time and who does it, and one idea so everyone feels welcome | — |
| Safe | The celebration and its origin in brief | Here and there: similarities and differences with a local celebration | Our day: activities and timetable | Roles, materials and one decision that makes the day inclusive |
| Daring | Origin and meaning | How it has changed over time, and how it compares with a local celebration | Our day: activities, timetable and roles | Materials, estimated cost, and why every choice is inclusive and avoids stereotypes |

In a group of 3 (Safe and Daring), Part 4 is shared: each member adds one of its points at the end of their part.

**Every student also writes the individual formal email** to the head teacher: Insecure guided template · Safe four paragraphs · Daring four paragraphs plus one concern of the head teacher answered.

## 5. How it is marked

| Row | Criteria | Who gets the mark | Weight |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. My part of the pitch | 3.1 | individual | 25 % |
| 2. My formal email | 5.1 | individual | 20 % |
| 3. Research & credits | 4.2, 6.1, 6.2 | group | 10 % |
| 4. Respect & inclusion | 1.1–1.3, CC1 1.3 | group | 10 % |
| 5. Event plan | CE2 emprenedora 2.3 | group | 15 % |
| 6. Teamwork (peer assessment) | 3.2 | individual | 10 % |
| 7. Preparation dossier | 3.2, 5.1 | individual | 10 % |

- Individual 65 %, group 35 %. Row 7: Level 4 = all five pieces, complete, with a real change after feedback; Level 3 = all five, one incomplete or changes not explained; Level 2 = three or four pieces; Level 1 = one or two pieces. Same scale as LS1: Level 4 = 10 · 3 = 7.5 · 2 = 5 · 1 = 2.5 · no evidence = 0.
- Rows 1–5 use the **route rubric** of the group (section 7).
- **Safety net:** if peer assessment and the teacher's observation show that a student did not contribute to the group product, the teacher lowers that student's group rows (3–5) by one level. Nobody loses marks for a teammate's part or email.

## 6. Peer assessment

**a) Inside the group: teamwork (marked, row 6).** After submitting, each student rates every teammate, and themselves, from 1 to 4 on three statements, anonymously:

1. Did their part of the research and the plan on time.
2. Listened to others and helped the group decide.
3. Helped the group to improve the slides and the video.

The average of the teammates' ratings (self-rating not counted) gives the level for row 6. The self-rating is compared with it in Reflect. If the ratings and the teacher's observation disagree a lot, the teacher decides.

**b) Between groups: feedback on the draft (not marked).** In the class before the deadline, each group watches another group's draft video and fills in a short form: two things that work and one thing to improve, using the rubric rows 3–5 and "every member speaks, face and slides visible". The group uses it to fix the video before submitting.

## 7. Rubrics by route

### Insecure

| Row | Level 4 | Level 3 | Level 2 | Level 1 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. My part | All the content of my part in clear short sentences (*It is celebrated in…*, *People…*, *At 10 o'clock…*); 30–45 s; I only glance at the slides. | All the content, but I read 1–2 full sentences or make 2–3 errors with *is celebrated* / Present Simple. | Some content missing, or I read most of my part. | 1–2 sentences only, I read everything, or I cannot be understood. |
| 2. Email | Template complete: formal greeting and ending, why I am writing, date, place and activities, one request; no contractions; at most 2 errors. | Complete with 3–4 errors, or one part very short. | One part missing, 5 or more errors, or informal words (*Hi*, *thanks*). | Most gaps empty, or not about the event. |
| 3. Research & credits | Information from 2 sources, correct, both credited on the last slide (title + website). | 2 sources, one credit incomplete. | Only 1 source credited, or a fact is wrong. | No credits, or copied text. |
| 4. Respect & inclusion | Customs explained as information (*Some people…*), no stereotypes or jokes; one clear idea so everyone feels welcome. | Respectful, but the welcome idea is vague (*everybody can come*). | One generalisation (*All Indian people…*) or no welcome idea. | Stereotypes, jokes about the tradition or caricature costumes. |
| 5. Event plan | Three realistic activities for school, each with a time and who does it. | Three activities; one has no time or no person. | Only 1–2 activities, or not realistic for school. | No plan. |

### Safe

| Row | Level 4 | Level 3 | Level 2 | Level 1 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. My part | All the content of my part, structured; LS grammar used well (passive, *used to*, future forms); 45–60 s; I speak naturally. | Clear and complete, but 1–2 read sentences or 3–4 grammar errors. | Content missing (e.g. only similarities), or I read most of my part. | Key content missing, I read everything, or I cannot be understood. |
| 2. Email | Four paragraphs (purpose, plan, request, closing); formal register; one polite request (*Could we…? / Would it be possible…?*); future forms and a relative clause; at most 3 errors. | All four paragraphs, 4–5 errors or one informal expression. | A paragraph missing, the request not polite, or 6 or more errors. | Not a formal email, or not about the event. |
| 3. Research & credits | Facts from 2 reliable sources, contrasted where they differ, both credited (title, website, year). | 2 sources credited but not contrasted, or one credit incomplete. | One unreliable source (shop, ad, no author), or only 1 source. | No credits, or copied text. |
| 4. Respect & inclusion | Fair comparison with the local celebration, beliefs as information, and at least one decision that makes the day inclusive, with a reason. | Respectful comparison; the inclusive decision has no reason. | One generalisation or stereotype, or no inclusive decision. | Stereotypes, mocking or caricature costumes. |
| 5. Event plan | Timetable, a role for every member and a list of materials, all realistic for school. | All three, one incomplete (e.g. one member without a role). | One of the three missing. | Only a list of ideas, no plan. |

### Daring

| Row | Level 4 | Level 3 | Level 2 | Level 1 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. My part | All the content of my part, argued and fluent, with linking words and accurate LS grammar (passive, *used to*, future forms, relative clauses); 60–90 s; no reading. | Complete and argued, 1–2 read sentences or a few errors that do not affect meaning. | Description without argument, or content missing, or I read most of my part. | Key content missing, I read everything, or I cannot be understood. |
| 2. Email | Four paragraphs in formal register (*I would be grateful if…*); one concern of the head teacher anticipated and answered; varied, accurate language; at most 2 errors. | The concern is answered weakly, or 3–4 errors. | No concern answered, one informal expression, or 5 or more errors. | Not a formal email, or not about the event. |
| 3. Research & credits | Facts from 2 or more reliable sources, contrasted and evaluated (who wrote it, why), summarised in our own words, all credited. | Reliable and credited, but not evaluated. | One unreliable source, or one credit missing. | No credits, or copied text. |
| 4. Respect & inclusion | Every choice (activities, food, clothes, music) justified as inclusive; beliefs as information; fair comparison with a local celebration; we explain how we avoid stereotypes. | Most choices justified; respectful throughout. | Few justifications, or one generalisation. | Stereotypes, mocking or caricature costumes. |
| 5. Event plan | Timetable, roles, materials and a realistic estimated cost (items and amounts). | All four, one incomplete or the cost has no items. | One of the four missing. | Only a list of ideas, no plan. |
