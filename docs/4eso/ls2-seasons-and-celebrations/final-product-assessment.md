## 1. Groups

- **Same-route groups** (Roger, 24 Oct 2026): every group is Insecure, Safe or Daring, and the whole product follows that route's requirements and rubric.
- **The teacher forms the groups** inside each route, not friends' choice, so pairs that do not work well can be separated.
- Size: Insecure groups of 3; Safe and Daring groups of 3–4. If a route has too few students for a group, those students join the nearest route's group and keep their own route for their part and email.
- Formed in **week 2 (2–6 Nov)**, before Learning Path 3, and the group chooses its celebration then. Pieces 2, 4 and 5 in the Learning Paths are about the group's celebration, so the groups must exist before them even though the final product itself is made in term 2.
- Support for Insecure groups: slide template, sentence frames for every part, Catalan guidance on the page, and a short check-in with the teacher every class.

## 2. Individual evidence during the team work

The group works as a team, but at **each step** every student hands in, on the Final Product page:

1. a **short individual task** (the evidence of what they did),
2. a **self-assessment**: 3–4 statements, each rated *1 Not yet · 2 Partly · 3 Mostly · 4 Fully*,
3. an optional **comment for the teacher**: *Anything you want to tell me? A problem you or your group faced?*

The dashboard shows, per student and step: done or not, the self-assessment average (e.g. 3.3 / 4) and a 💬 when there is a comment; clicking opens the task text, the ratings and the comment. The texts are already synced; only the view is new. Insecure sees the statements in Catalan.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 · Before the team work | Pieces 1–5 from the Learning Paths, already filled in (a missing one must be written or pasted), plus *what I changed after the feedback, and why* for pieces 1, 2 and 5 | I have all five pieces · I improved my texts after the feedback · I know what my group's celebration is about | Learning Path work done |

| 2 · Plan and slides (week 6) | **My contribution:** my role, the activity or part of the plan I prepared, the text of my slide(s) | I did my part on time · My activity is realistic for school · My slide has short text and an image · Our plan includes everyone | 3.2, 1.2 |

- The self-assessment does **not** change the mark by itself: it shows the teacher how each student sees their own work, and the gap between self-assessment and teacher assessment is discussed in Reflect.
- A student who has not handed in steps 1 and 2 records their part only after doing so.
- Steps 0–5 together are row 5 (*My process evidence*), and they are what the teacher uses to adjust group rows for a student who did not contribute.

## 3. What the group makes together

1. **Research card** (Learning Path 4, piece 3): key information, reliable or not with two reasons and the credit line for each source (Insecure two, Safe and Daring three), where the sources agree or differ, the most trusted one, and one stereotype to avoid.
2. **Event plan** for a school celebration day, at the route's level.
3. **Google Slides:** one or two slides per part, short text and images, credits on the last slide.
4. **Video:** every member presents their part, face and slides visible. The video lasts 2–3 minutes: each part lasts 40–60 s in Insecure groups and 35–45 s in Safe and Daring groups.

## 4. Who presents what (each student owns one part)

| Route | Part 1 | Part 2 | Part 3 | Part 4 (groups of 4) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Insecure | What, when and where; what it means for the people who celebrate it and why it belongs at school | Three customs, one food or symbol, and one thing in common with a local celebration | Our day: three activities, each with a time and who does it; one decision so it is for everyone; one stereotype we avoid (why it is unfair and how) | — |
| Safe | The celebration, its origin in brief, what it means for the people who celebrate it and why it belongs at school | Here and there: similarities and differences with a local celebration | Our day: activities and timetable | Roles, materials, one decision that makes the day inclusive, and one stereotype we avoid (why it is unfair and how) |
| Daring | Origin, what it means for the people who celebrate it and why it belongs at school | How it has changed over time, and how it compares with a local celebration | Our day: activities, timetable and roles | Materials, estimated cost, why every choice is inclusive, and one stereotype our plan challenges |

In a group of 3 (Safe and Daring), Part 4 is shared: each member adds one of its points at the end of their part.

**Every student also writes the individual formal email** to the head teacher, with at least two concrete actions so every student and family feels welcome: Insecure guided template · Safe four paragraphs · Daring four paragraphs, the welcome actions with reasons, plus one concern of the head teacher answered.

Before (24 Oct, proposed): one weighted rubric of nine rows (part 25, email 15, welcome 5, research 10, meaning 5, local link 10, stereotypes 10, teamwork 10, process 10), where the group rows made 35 % of the final product mark.

- Row 5: Level 4 = every step handed in on time, specific, in the student's own words, with an honest self-assessment; Level 3 = every step, one late or very general; Level 2 = one or two steps missing; Level 1 = most steps missing. Same scale as LS1: Level 4 = 10 · 3 = 7.5 · 2 = 5 · 1 = 2.5 · no evidence = 0 (Level 1 = NA, 2 = AS, 3 = AN, 4 = AE).
- Rows use the **route rubric** of the group (section 7).
- **Safety net:** if peer assessment and the teacher's observation show that a student did not contribute to the group product, the teacher lowers that student's group rows (7–10) by one level. Nobody loses marks for a teammate's part or email.

## 6. Peer assessment

**a) Inside the group: teamwork (marked, row 4).** After submitting, each student rates every teammate, and themselves, from 1 to 4 on three statements, anonymously:

1. Did their part of the research and the plan on time.
2. Listened to others and helped the group decide.
3. Helped the group to improve the slides and the video.

The average of the teammates' ratings (self-rating not counted) gives the level for row 4. The self-rating is compared with it in Reflect. If the ratings and the teacher's observation disagree a lot, the teacher decides.

**b) Between groups: feedback on the draft (not marked).** In the class before the deadline, each group watches another group's draft video and fills in a short form: two things that work and one thing to improve, using the rubric rows 7–10 and "every member speaks, face and slides visible". The group uses it to fix the video before submitting.

## 7. Rubrics by route

The level descriptors are in `4eso/seasons-and-celebrations/final-rubric-data.js` (Rubric tab of the final product page; Insecure in Catalan, Safe and Daring in English). They follow the indicators in [indicators.md](indicators.md): Level 1 = NA, Level 2 = AS, Level 3 = AN, Level 4 = AE.

- The indicators are the same for every route. What changes is the task and the help: Insecure has the templates, Catalan guidance, two sources and 40–60 s parts; Safe and Daring have three sources and 35–45 s parts; Daring adds argued parts, an estimated cost, reasons for the welcome actions and the head teacher's concern.
- Rows 3–10 (my source, teamwork, process, welcome actions, research, meaning, local link, stereotypes) are shared by Safe and Daring; rows 1 and 2 have route-specific descriptors. Each row shows on the page whether it counts in the final product (its %) or in the other competences.
