# LS2 · Final product rubric (Celebration Day Proposal) · DRAFT

Status: **proposal, 24 Oct 2026, awaiting Roger's approval.** Not on the site yet. Once approved it goes into `4eso/seasons-and-celebrations/final-rubric-data.js` with the shared renderer `shared/js/final-rubric.js` (same format as LS1), with the Insecure rubric in Catalan.

## How it works

- Same scale as LS1: each row gets a level. Level 4 = 10 · Level 3 = 7.5 · Level 2 = 5 · Level 1 = 2.5 · no evidence = 0. Then the row's percentage is applied.
- Rows follow the five lines of the "What counts in the final assessment?" box on the LS2 page, so students see the same structure twice.
- **Individual or group:** the pitch (each student's own speaking) and the email are marked per student. Research, inclusion and the event plan are marked once for the group and shared, because the group builds them together.
- Percentages are a proposal (the LS2 page shows none yet). Individual work weighs 55 %, group work 45 %.

## Insecure

Route requirement: describe one celebration in short sentences (what, when, where, three customs, one food or symbol); three activities, each with a time and who does it; one idea so everyone feels welcome; email from the guided template. No comparison with a local celebration needed.

| Row | Level 4 | Level 3 | Level 2 | Level 1 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Pitch | I say what, when, where, three customs and one food or symbol in clear short sentences (*It is celebrated in…*, *People…*); I speak for my part, my face and the slides are visible, and I only glance at the slides. | All the information is there, but I read 1–2 full sentences or have 2–3 errors with *is celebrated* / Present Simple. | Some information is missing (only 1–2 customs, or no when/where), or I read most of my part. | I say only 1–2 sentences, read everything, or cannot be understood. |
| 2. Email | Template complete: formal greeting and ending, why I am writing, date, place and three activities, one request; no contractions; at most 2 errors. | Template complete with 3–4 errors, or one part (request or activities) is very short. | One part of the template is missing, or there are 5 or more errors, or informal words (*Hi*, *thanks*). | Most gaps are empty or the email is not about the event. |
| 3. Research & credits | Information from 2 sources, correct, and both credited on the last slide (title + website). | 2 sources, but one credit is incomplete (no title or no website). | Only 1 source credited, or a fact is wrong. | No credits, or text copied from a source. |
| 4. Respect & inclusion | Customs explained as information (*Some people believe…*), no stereotypes or jokes; one clear idea so everyone feels welcome. | Respectful, but the welcome idea is vague (*everybody can come*). | One generalisation (*All Indian people…*) or no welcome idea. | Stereotypes, jokes about the tradition or caricature costumes. |
| 5. Event plan | Three realistic activities for school, each with a time and who does it. | Three activities; one has no time or no person. | Only 1–2 activities, or not realistic for school. | No plan. |

## Safe

Route requirement: describe the celebration and compare it with a local one (similarities and differences); explain its origin briefly; timetable, roles and materials; at least one decision that makes the event inclusive; a full formal email in paragraphs.

| Row | Level 4 | Level 3 | Level 2 | Level 1 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Pitch | My part is structured and clear: I describe, compare (at least 1 similarity and 1 difference) and use the LS grammar (passive *is celebrated*, *used to* for origins, future forms for the plan); I speak naturally, only glancing at the slides. Slides: short text, images, one idea per slide. | Clear and complete, but I read 1–2 full sentences, or 3–4 grammar errors. | Only similarities or only differences, or I read most of my part, or slides with long paragraphs. | My part is missing key content, I read everything, or I cannot be understood. |
| 2. Email | Four paragraphs (purpose, plan, request, closing); formal register; at least one polite request (*Could we…? / Would it be possible…?*); future forms and a relative clause used correctly; at most 3 errors. | All four paragraphs, 4–5 errors or one informal expression. | A paragraph is missing, or the request is not polite, or 6 or more errors. | Not a formal email, or not about the event. |
| 3. Research & credits | Facts from 2 reliable sources, contrasted where they differ, both credited (title, website, date). | 2 sources credited but not contrasted, or one credit incomplete. | One source is unreliable (shop, ad, no author), or only 1 source. | No credits, or copied text. |
| 4. Respect & inclusion | Fair comparison with the local celebration, beliefs presented as information, and at least one decision that makes the event inclusive with a reason. | Respectful comparison; the inclusive decision has no reason. | One generalisation or stereotype, or no inclusive decision. | Stereotypes, mocking or caricature costumes. |
| 5. Event plan | Timetable, roles for every member and a list of materials; all realistic for school. | All three parts, one incomplete (e.g. no roles for one member). | One of the three parts is missing. | Only a list of ideas, no plan. |

## Daring

Route requirement: origin, meaning and change over time; timetable, roles, materials and an estimated cost; every choice justified so the event is inclusive, with no stereotypes or caricature costumes; the email answers one concern from the head teacher.

| Row | Level 4 | Level 3 | Level 2 | Level 1 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Pitch | I explain origin, meaning and change over time and argue why our event is a good idea; fluent, with linking words and the LS grammar used accurately (passive, *used to*, future forms, relative clauses); no reading. | Complete and well argued, with 1–2 read sentences or a few errors that do not affect meaning. | Description without argument, or change over time missing, or I read most of my part. | Key content missing, I read everything, or I cannot be understood. |
| 2. Email | Four paragraphs in formal register (*I would be grateful if…*); one concern from the head teacher anticipated and answered; varied, accurate language; at most 2 errors. | The concern is mentioned but answered weakly, or 3–4 errors. | No concern answered, or one informal expression, or 5 or more errors. | Not a formal email, or not about the event. |
| 3. Research & credits | Facts from 2 or more reliable sources, contrasted and evaluated (who wrote it, why); all credited; information summarised in our own words. | Sources credited and reliable, but not evaluated. | One unreliable source, or one credit missing. | No credits, or copied text. |
| 4. Respect & inclusion | Every choice (activities, food, clothes, music) justified as inclusive; beliefs presented as information; we explain how the event avoids stereotypes. | Most choices justified; respectful throughout. | Few justifications, or one generalisation. | Stereotypes, mocking or caricature costumes. |
| 5. Event plan | Timetable, roles, materials and a realistic estimated cost (what we need and how much). | All four parts, one incomplete or the cost is a guess with no items. | One of the four parts is missing. | Only a list of ideas, no plan. |
