Every page keeps the full dropdown in its HTML; for a student (and the tester in a group's view) `lsMenu` in `el-cloud.js` removes the other courses, the "Soon" group and the LSs of their course that are hidden or not assigned to their group (Roger, 26 Oct 2026), so a new LS needs no extra code there; the teacher and the tester's full view see everything.
- Use each course’s `my-real-routine/getting-started.html` as the shell/topbar reference. Reuse `shared/css/layout.css`, `topbar.css`, `activities.css` and existing shared helpers rather than creating a new visual system.

On pages with that selector, `lp-theme-2eso.css`/`lp-theme-4eso.css` hide the brand number and title; keep the markup, and keep the selector's options complete and ordered (`section-next.js` reads them for "continue to the next Learning Path").
- **Class presentations (grammar and vocabulary decks):** one self-contained page per deck in `lab/grammar/<course>-<topic>.html` (grammar) or `lab/vocabulary/<course>-<topic>.html` (vocabulary), in the course palette, listed in the English Lab Grammar or Vocabulary menu (`lab/index.html`) and linked from its Learning Path page with an `a.deck-link` and `?from=<that page>` (the ← button goes back there). **Vocabulary introductions (Roger, 25 Oct 2026):** the class guesses each new word first (a clue, a picture emoji or a sentence), then the reveal shows the word, a 🔊 button that says it with the browser's English voice (`speechSynthesis`, en-GB; on the projector or on each student's Chromebook) and its Catalan meaning, with a short reason; each section ends with a word wall or the pattern (sun → sunny). Reference: `lab/vocabulary/4eso-autumn-words.html` (the words of Getting Started, 4 ESO Seasons & Celebrations), built on the same engine as the grammar reference deck. **Question-first (Roger, 25 Oct 2026):** lecture decks made him talk too much, so new decks ask the class before they explain: every question shows how students answer (✋ 1–3 fingers, 👍/👎, ✏️ write it, 💬 turn and talk, 🎲 random class-list number, 🏆 team points) and → / Space / a presenter clicker reveals one step at a time (answer, then rule). Reference, a prototype awaiting Roger's feedback: `lab/grammar/4eso-present-simple-customs.html` (`data-step` shows an element, `data-open` reveals its answer, `data-until` removes it, `data-label` names the step on the Next button, `data-ca` holds the Catalan help). Never put student names in a deck: the picker uses class-list numbers. **Every presentation explains the team points (Roger, 25 Oct 2026):** right after the cover it has `<section class="slide" data-el-points></section>` (the reminder: the rules and a quick check, filled by `shared/js/el-deck-points.js`), the 🏆 Teams strip (`#teambar` with `data-course`, `#teamsBtn`) and, before the deck's own script, `shared/js/el-sound.js`, `shared/js/el-points.js` and `shared/js/el-deck-points.js`. **Sound effects (Roger, 25 Oct 2026):** `shared/js/el-sound.js` makes them with the Web Audio API (no sound files): `ELSound.play(name)` for the 3 · 2 · 1 countdown (`tick`, `go`), the random number (`pick`), the timer's end (`time`), + on the team strip (`point`) and the buzzer (`open`, `first`, `right`, `wrong` on the projector; `press`, `win` on students' devices); the deck's 🔊 tool or M mutes them in that browser (`el_sound`). See "Team points" under the teacher dashboard. **Every deck engine keeps the live hooks** (copy them from the reference deck): after each step it dispatches `deckstep` with `info()`; it exposes `window.ELDeck = {info, show, setCa}`; options carry `data-i` (their place before shuffling) and `info().order` sends each slide's order as a string ("2,1,0": Firestore refuses nested arrays); and while `live` is set (`.deck-live` on the root) it hides the nav buttons, tools and rail and ignores the student's keys except C (Catalan) and F (full screen). `#teambar` also has `data-ls` (the LS whose route decides Catalan). See "Buzzer and live view".
- **Class games (Roger, 26 Oct 2026: "like a Kahoot or a Blooket"):** one engine in `lab/game/`, and a game is only content. A new game = a deck file `lab/game/decks/<course>-<topic>.js` that calls `ELGameDeck({title, slides})` plus one line in `lab/game/decks/index.js` (`{id, title, course, emoji, ls}`). Slides, in order: theory `{theory, img, lines:[…]}` (shown on the board only) and question `{q, img, options:[2–4], why}` where **the first option is the right one** (the board shuffles them, the same order on every reload; `keep:true` keeps the order, e.g. True / False). Text: `**word**` is highlighted, `___` is a gap; `img` is an emoji or an image file. No timer: only right or wrong. **Every game follows these rules (Roger, 26 Oct 2026):** it is at **Safe** level (not easy), and it uses the learning paths' vocabulary in two ways: **passive** (the words appear in the questions and theory texts) and **active** (questions that ask for a word: meaning, use, collocation or preposition). Choose mainly the words that are not common at their level: the [S] lists of the LS overview (`docs/<course>/ls…/overview.md`), not the basic [I] ones. The teacher opens the board (`lab/game/host.html`, also linked from the dashboard's Points box), chooses the game and the group; from then on `host.js` publishes it in `live/{group}` (`game:true`, phase lobby | theory | q | reveal | end, the question and its options but never the right one before it is shown) and every signed-in student of that group is taken from any page into the player (`lab/game/play.html?g=…&from=…`: `el-cloud.js` and `el-deck-points.js` watch `live/{group}`). The player loads no deck file: it shows only the question and four coloured tiles (red ▲, blue ◆, gold ●, green ■), one tap, then green ✓ or red ✗. Answers go to `live/{group}/answers/{email}` and right answers become the same term points as the presentations (`points/{email}`, `teams/{group}.best`; bonus up to +0.5 in proportion to the group's best, `el-points.js`; the dashboard's Points box exports them as CSV). The answer shows by itself once everyone has answered. Music (`ELGame.music`, Web Audio, no files) plays on the board only; students' devices play only their own tap and result sounds. Reference decks: `lab/game/decks/4eso-customs-dates.js`, `lab/game/decks/2eso-free-time.js`. **Theory slides go between the questions** (about 3 theory slides and 15–20 questions), each followed by questions that practise it. Roger's readable guide: `docs/general/class-games-guide.md` (keep it in step with these rules, add each new game to its table, and update the copy in his Drive, Feina > Teacher Studio). **Each game is linked from its learning path for the teacher only** (Roger, 26 Oct 2026): on the page of the learning path whose content it closes, a `<p data-el-teacher hidden>` link to `lab/game/host.html?deck=<id>` (el-cloud.js shows `[data-el-teacher]` only to the teacher's account); it opens the board on that game, ready to choose the group. The game uses the live and answers documents of the class quiz. Testers answer and get points too (to try it), but their points never set the group's best: the best is taken over the student list only.

## Route language and scaffolding
- **Beginners (Roger, 26 Oct 2026):** a fourth route, A1, below Insecure, for students starting almost from zero. Everything Insecure gets in Catalan, plus heavier scaffolding: a short core word list per LS practised with spaced retrieval (flashcards and recall every session), readings of 60–100 words built from that list at 95–98 % known words with Catalan hover glosses, multiple choice or matching instead of open answers, the slow audio copy with English subtitles from the first viewing, sentence frames with gaps and a word bank, the same final product with fewer requirements, feedback as a Catalan hint and a second try, and a short "how did I do it?" box. Same listenings and videos as everyone, mixed pairs in class (not Beginners with Beginners). Marked on the same NA–AE scale against CEFR A1 can-do statements. **Only the teacher sets it** (`pick:false` in `EL_LEVELS`: students cannot choose or request it; `firestore.rules` unchanged, the teacher may write any route). **In code:** `elCa(level)` (`el-catalog.js`) says whether a route reads Catalan; LS2 engines order routes `beginners < insecure < safe < daring`, an untagged item is for every route, and a text, list or rubric with no `beginners` version falls back to `insecure` (`byLevel`, `forLevel`, `final-rubric.js`, the marking PDF, the study PDF). Every LS2 Learning Path page has a Beginners route button (students only see their own). LS1 pages have no Beginners version: `applyLevel` shows them the Insecure one. New LS content adds `beginners` keys where the A1 version differs (readings, questions, tasks, rubric), designed in the LS project. **Seasons & Celebrations (4 ESO) and Free Time, Live! (2 ESO) have Beginners content on every page since 26 Oct 2026** (docs/4eso/ls2-seasons-and-celebrations/overview.md and docs/2eso/ls2-free-time-live/overview.md, section "Beginners route"): the convention is that untagged items are for every route and `r: 'insecure'` marks what starts at Insecure. **The programmes** (docs/4eso/programme.md and docs/2eso/programme.md) carry the Beginners route since 26 Oct 2026 (Roger approved): a Beginners product bullet, core word list and A1 grammar block per LS, and a final chapter "Resum de gramàtica i lèxic · ruta Beginners (A1)" that is the reference for designing Beginners content in any LS.
- **Insecure (and Beginners):** pedagogical instructions, explanations, strategy tips, checklists, step-by-step guidance and corrective feedback are in **Catalan**. Use clear scaffolding and worked examples; explain briefly what went wrong and what to notice.
- **Safe:** instructions and explanations are in **English**. Catalan may appear only as limited vocabulary support, such as hover/focus meanings.
- **Daring:** English-first, with greater learner autonomy and less scaffolding.
- **English remains the target language** for vocabulary, listening/reading input, model sentences, interview questions where appropriate, slide content and student production. Do not translate these into Catalan when adding scaffolding.
- Reuse route-aware support such as `shared/js/learning-path-support.js` where appropriate; verify language updates correctly when switching routes.

## Levels, listenings, videos and readings
- Each route has the level agreed in the levels document of the teaching programme:

  | Course | Year horizon | Beginners | Insecure | Safe | Daring |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | 1ESO | A1 consolidated; starting A2 tasks | Pre-A1 / A1 with a lot of support | A1 with a lot of support | A1 consolidated | A1+ / early A2 |
  | 2ESO | Functional A2 | A1 with a lot of support | A1+ / early A2 with scaffolding | Functional A2 | A2+ / emerging B1 |
  | 3ESO | More autonomous A2; towards B1 | A1 with a lot of support | A2 with support | More autonomous A2 | Emerging B1 |
  | 4ESO | B1 as the final horizon | A1 with a lot of support | A2 / A2+ with support | Indicative B1 | B1 / B1+ |

  Content marked as an extension of the next level (for example B1+ in 4ESO) goes only to Daring. This school year the site covers **2ESO and 4ESO** only.
- **Listenings and videos are pitched at the Safe level**, and every route hears the same input. Do not write separate scripts per route.
- **The activities around that input adapt to each route's level:** questions, tasks, support, hints and the language of instructions. Insecure gets easier questions, support in Catalan and, where possible, a slower version of the audio; Beginners gets the slow copy with subtitles from the first viewing and multiple choice or matching only. Daring gets the more demanding questions (inference, comparison, opinion).
- **Reading texts are different: they vary by route.** Each route reads a version written at its own level (for 4ESO: A2 / A2+ for Insecure, B1 for Safe, B1 / B1+ for Daring), on the same topic and with the same key information, so the whole class can share the content. Adapt length, sentence complexity, vocabulary and grammar to the level; the questions follow the route's text.
- **Per-route readings in code:** in pages built with `ls2-engine.js`, `media.text` is a string or `{beginners, insecure, safe, daring}` (`beginners` optional: without it Beginners reads the Insecure text); standalone pages keep one text per route in a `readingData` / `readingChat` object keyed by level. Questions stay cumulative (`r:`), so every question a route sees must be answerable from that route's text.
- **Scripted videos:** one MP4 at Safe level plus a slower copy for Insecure, stored next to the page in `video/`, with WebVTT subtitles (`.vtt`) that students switch on after a first viewing. The slow copy is made locally with ffmpeg (`atempo=0.85`, longer pauses), never with a second paid generation. Scripts are recorded in the LS docs before production.
- Mix real, level-appropriate listenings with generated ones. Never call a paid generation service (for example ElevenLabs) or spend its credits without the user's explicit permission for that specific generation. An approved script is not permission to generate.
- **Generated audio files:** MP3s live in the LS folder's `audio/` (for example `4eso/seasons-and-celebrations/audio/culture-bites-origins.mp3`) and are referenced from the LS data as `media.audio`. The slow version for Insecure sits next to it with a `-slow` suffix (`culture-bites-origins-slow.mp3`). Both files are also copied to that LS's `Listenings` folder in Roger's Drive in the same task (see the Drive rules at the top).
- **ElevenLabs settings:** for new audio use the expressive model `eleven_v3` with audio tags in the script (`[excited]`, `[surprised]`, `[laughs]`…), shown to the user before generating; recordings made before 24 Oct 2026 used `eleven_multilingual_v2` without tags. One call per script line (one voice per character), lines joined with 0.45 s pauses and loudness-normalised (`loudnorm=I=-16:TP=-1.5`, mono, 96 kbps). Keep the same voice for a character across an LS; each LS's `AGENTS.md` lists its voices.
- **Choosing voices for new audio:** shortlist 3–4 well-rated ElevenLabs Voice Library voices per character (right accent and age, educational or conversational use case, high usage count) and send the user their free preview clips; generate only after the user picks. Existing recordings keep their voices.
- **Slow versions are always made locally, never with a second paid generation:** apply ffmpeg `atempo=0.85` to the same line clips and join them with 0.9 s pauses.

## Writing and speaking revisions
- **Revision counts (Roger, 26 Oct 2026, research audit):** in LS2 engine pages (`ls2-engine.js`), the first version a student sends for AI feedback is kept as the draft (`state.drafts[step_route] = {text, at}`; speaking: the transcript of that recording). The step completes only when they save an improved final version with a line on what they changed (`state.finals[step_route] = {text, changed, at}`; it must differ from the draft, and the line needs 3+ words). The final version is the product piece. The student sees their draft above the final version; the teacher sees draft, final version and "What I changed" in the activity dialog (`revisions` in `teacher.html`). Catalan for Insecure and Beginners.
- **Core words and dictation chunks (Roger, 26 Oct 2026, research audit):** each LS2 engine vocabulary panel has `core: {insecure:[…], safe:[…], daring:[…]}`: 15–20 words per route (all of them when the route sees fewer), the English text exactly as an item the route sees (`forLevel` rules; Beginners falls back to Insecure, so add `beginners` only for a shorter A1 list). Core words are for the Catalan→English flashcards and come back in later readings, warm-ups and checkpoints; the rest stay receptive. Each listening panel (`listen`, and the `chart` step) has `chunks: [...]`: 2–3 chunks of 3–6 words copied verbatim from `media.script` or the video `.vtt`, for the micro-dictation. Sample exams have none. A new LS adds both (playbook).
- **Goal and review (Roger, 26 Oct 2026, research audit):** every LS2 engine Learning Path (pages with a `recall` panel) and 4ESO Seasons & Celebrations `getting-started.html` (its own renderer, so it keeps a copy of the cards and the tracking note in `renderPlan()`; change both together) opens with a one-line "My goal" card and ends with "How did it go?" (what helped most, what to review), saved as `state.plan[route]` and `state.review[route]`, never a step and never marked; the teacher reads them in the activity dialog.

## Catalan-to-English cards and micro-dictation
- **Study cards have two directions (Roger, 26 Oct 2026, research audit):** English → Català (as before) and Català → English, where the card shows the Catalan and the student types or says the English before turning it (`state.cardDir`). The Catalan → English deck uses the vocab panel's `core` list (English words exactly as in the items, or one list per route) and every word of the Learning Path until the LS project adds it. Both LS2 engines.
- **Micro-dictation:** a listening panel (`type: 'mc'` with media, or a listening chart `type: 'step'`) that has `chunks: ['…', '…']` (2–3 bits of 3–6 words, exactly as in the script, chosen in the LS project) shows a dictation card after its questions: the browser's English voice says each chunk (normal and slower) and the student writes it. Practice only, not a step, not marked; answers in `state.dict[panel]`. No `chunks`, no card.
- 4ESO Seasons & Celebrations `getting-started.html` has its own renderer: it keeps copies of both (`renderCaCards`, `renderDict`), reading `core` from its `words` panel and `chunks` from its `listen` panel in `data/getting-started.js`. Change both places together.

## Grammar
- Grammar content is specific to each Learning Situation. Do not assume the same grammar across future Learning Situations.
- Follow the grammar requirements defined for the current LS.
- Differentiate routes mainly through scaffolding, complexity, amount of support and learner independence.

## Vocabulary and matching
- Vocabulary reference: `2eso/my-real-routine/a-day-in-my-life.html`. Reuse thematic groups (`.vocab-group`), compact expression pills (`.chip`) and hover **and keyboard-focus** support (`.chip-tooltip`) showing a Catalan meaning plus an English example. Adapt through the existing course palette.
- Matching tests only expressions explicitly studied in the route, cumulatively where appropriate. Do not accept untaught combinations merely because they sound natural in English.
- Judge the studied expression, not the original item ID or position. Identical left-side tiles must be interchangeable.
- Curate or validate generated sets so every round remains completable and pedagogically unambiguous; cumulative routes must meaningfully cover newly introduced vocabulary.
- Represent alternatives explicitly and naturally; avoid generic slash-splitting, awkward fragments and incomplete expression templates.
- Tell students to match studied Learning Path expressions even when other English combinations sound natural, and that identical tiles can be used interchangeably. Apply the route-language rules above.

## Flashcards
- Flashcard practice uses a **random set of up to 10 cards** (all cards if the deck has fewer). Do not make students work through the whole deck.
- The student **flips** the card, then chooses **Got it** or **Again**. The rating buttons stay disabled until the card is flipped. An **Again** card comes back before the student moves on, and the step is complete when every card in the set is **Got it**.
- "New set" gives a different random set. The counter keeps the format `Card 6 of 10 · Practising · 5/10 known`; Insecure shows it and the instructions in Catalan.
- Reuse the existing implementations (`flashcard-set` logic in the vocabulary pages and `shared/js/flashcard-set.js`) instead of writing a new one.

## Answer options
- **Randomize the answer options, never the questions.** Multiple-choice options and dropdown options are shuffled every time a question is shown, so the correct answer is not always in the same position. Question order stays as written.
- Use `learningSupport.shuffled()` / `learningSupport.shuffledIndexes()` from `shared/js/learning-path-support.js` (include that file in every page that renders questions). Keep each option's original value so scoring and feedback do not change.
- Do not change the wording of questions or options when shuffling.

## Assessment and grading
- **Every LS criterion ends with a degree** (NA, AS, AN, AE) that the teacher can justify with its indicators (`indicators.md` of the LS) and a piece of work. The level comes first; the mark is worked out from it. Each LS documents its evidence per criterion in a `grading.md`; worked example: [4ESO LS2](docs/4eso/ls2-seasons-and-celebrations/grading.md), also in Roger's reference doc *Worked example: from competence to LS criterion*.
- **LS mark (Roger, 24 Oct 2026):** final exam 35 % · final product 25 % · grammar and vocabulary activities 20 % · other competences 20 %, recorded from every activity. Key competences are the communicative skills (CE2 Listening, CE3 Speaking, CE4 Reading, CE5 Writing): assessed in the exam and the final product. The other competences (CE1, CE6–CE10, transversal) form the 20 % block. **Grammar and vocabulary block (Roger, 26 Oct 2026):** inside the grammar and vocabulary block, recall checkpoints 50 % (first try of all four) · recall warm-ups 25 % (first try) · practice, grammar and mixed practice done by the session deadline 25 %. Accuracy only comes from delayed retrieval, never from first tries on brand-new practice, so students can retry practice freely (research audit, Roger 26 Oct 2026: https://claude.ai/artifact/FhBnrKpdnaYA5C4bjDoBbT); `gv.parts` in `el-grading.js`.
- **Collecting the data:** the site records completion and first tries automatically; the teacher gives one-click levels only at a few planned moments, on work already read for feedback or a mark. Scores alone never set the degree of a criterion whose indicators describe a task. With two pieces of evidence the later one weighs more; the dashboard suggests, the teacher confirms.
- **Scale:** NA 2.5 · AS 5 · AN 7.5 · AE 10 · no evidence 0. Mark to level: under 5 NA · 5–6.9 AS · 7–8.9 AN · 9–10 AE.
