⏱ The 10-second check

Before you send a message in English, look at each verb:

  1. Does it need a little word? add to, explain to, on the list, in the table, at the top.
  2. Does it agree? these + plural, he / she / it + -s, feedback / info with no -s.
  3. Is anyone missing? Every verb needs a subject (it happens) and want / allow need someone to.
  4. Is it a question? Auxiliary first: Can students…? Does it…? But not inside a sentence: I know where it is.
  5. Would a native say it? If it’s a word-for-word Catalan phrase, check ✨ Make it idiomatic.

Grammar & discourse

  • Questions: auxiliary before the subject; indirect questions keep statement order; tag questions
  • Adverb position: already, always, only, exactly before the main verb
  • still / yet; present perfect with yet and already
  • want / ask / allow + someone + to; let / make + verb; recommend + that or -ing
  • Present continuous and perfect for now: I’m attaching, I’ve just uploaded
  • One subject per verb: no dummy it, no missing it
  • Phrasal verbs: the pronoun in the middle (figure it out); three-part verbs never split
  • Narrative tenses: past simple, past continuous, past perfect, present perfect
  • Articles, uncountable nouns, a / an by sound, these / criteria / -s, compounds and ’s

Vocabulary & expressions

  • on / in / at with devices, apps, places on a screen, modes and levels
  • Fixing things: figure out, pin down, get to the bottom of, work around, iron out, it turns out, that did the trick
  • make vs do collocations
  • Keeping going: put off, get around to, chase up, deal with, get it over with, not sold on
  • Telling a story: come up with, get off the ground, get the hang of it, hit a wall, take off, end up, run itself
  • School English: next school year, the school, master document, indicators, hand in, cover a class

ⓘ About this course

🎯 What is it for?

Your English is clear and fluent, but much of it is still built in Catalan and then translated: here it happens the same, I want that you, in my phone. This course is made only from your own sentences (about 3,000 messages and their corrections) and the expressions in your vocabulary sheet. Only grammar and idiom count: typing slips, capitals and punctuation never do.

🧭 How every learning path works

  1. 🔍 Spot the mistake: your own sentences, one mistake each.
  2. Vocabulary with flashcards (both directions) and gap practice.
  3. Reading and Listening: C1 texts and recordings built from the path’s vocabulary, with a micro-dictation.
  4. 🎬 Movie dictation: short parts of real film scenes; type the missing words.
  5. Catalan → English and Grammar: the structures you get wrong, typed and checked.
  6. ✨ Make it idiomatic: your own sentences, rewritten the native way, typed or said aloud, then compared and heard.
  7. 🗣 Shadowing: the corrected sentences in the Heart voice, sentence by sentence (Listen and repeat) or all along.
  8. Writing: handed in for correction, back in the same tab.
  9. ⏱ 4 / 3 / 2 fluency: one Speaking question told three times, each time with less time, so the chunks get automatic.
  10. 🎙 Speaking: 4 random questions from a pool of 12, recorded; the recording is transcribed and corrected, and the feedback appears in the tab.
  11. 🔁 Speak again: the same questions, answered again with the mistakes fixed.

🌱 How it grows

Every new list of corrections becomes a new batch in 5 · My latest mistakes (spot it, fix it, Catalan → English, make it idiomatic, spoken English, shadowing). The sentences Roig corrects in your 🎙 Speaking answers go into the next batch too, so the course also learns from your spoken English. 📍 in / on / at collects every preposition slip, and 🗓 This week’s mix brings back 12 mistakes from all your lists every Monday. After three newer lists, an old one moves to 🔁 Review, mixed with the others. New themes can become new learning paths.
