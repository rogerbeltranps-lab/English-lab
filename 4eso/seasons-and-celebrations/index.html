Your challenge

A Celebration Day for our school

In groups of 3–4, design a respectful celebration day at school around one tradition. Record a 2–3 minute video pitch with Google Slides (everyone speaks, faces and slides visible together) and write a formal email to the head teacher proposing the event.

What will you learn?

Learning objectives of this Learning Situation:

  • O1 · Interpret written and spoken texts about seasons, traditions and celebrations (articles, short videos, people explaining how they celebrate), identifying main ideas, details and meaning from context, in order to build the background knowledge the project needs. (CE4 · 4.1 + CE2 · 2.2)
  • O2 · Check information about the chosen celebration in at least two sources, assessing their reliability and crediting them, in order to communicate accurate and respectful content. (CE6 · 6.1 + CE4 · 4.2)
  • O3 · Explain the customs, dates and origins of a celebration and what it means for the people who celebrate it, using Present Simple, “it is celebrated / it takes place”, Past Simple and used to, in order to present it to an audience that does not know it. (CE3 · 3.2 + CE1 · 1.1)
  • O4 · Compare the chosen celebration with a local one, building links between cultures and rejecting stereotypes, in order to make the school event inclusive. Deeper in Safe and Daring. (CE1 · 1.2 + CC3 · 3.2)
  • O5 · Plan a school celebration day using future forms (going to, will, Present Continuous) and relative clauses, in order to propose it to the head teacher in a formal email. (CE5 · 5.1 + CC1 · 1.3)

How will we work?

Read & notice → Practise words → Listen & compare → Research → Create & share → Reflect.

  • Remember before you look: every Learning Path opens with a recall quiz without notes.
  • Again and again: words and grammar come back in later Learning Paths and in weekly recall checkpoints.
  • Mix it up: practice mixes new and old language, so you learn to choose the right form.
  • Feedback and a second try: a step is done when you get 80 % right.
  • Build your product step by step: each Learning Path gives you one piece of your final product.

Curriculum competences

This Learning Situation develops the Catalan curriculum for Foreign Language (Decree 175/2022). The four communication skills are worked in every LS; this one also integrates intercultural awareness and research, and two citizenship competences.

  • CE4 · Reading: understand and interpret texts about celebrations, and judge how reliable a source is.
  • CE2 · Listening: interpret and value what people say about how and why they celebrate.
  • CE3 · Speaking: plan, rehearse and record the group video pitch together.
  • CE5 · Writing: write a formal email to the head teacher proposing the event.
  • CE1 · Intercultural awareness: value cultural diversity, build links between cultures and reject stereotypes.
  • CE6 · Research & information: search, select and contrast sources, and credit them.
  • Citizenship (CC1 and CC3): propose actions for living together, and take an active part in overcoming stereotypes and prejudice.

Assessment criteria

Each assessment criterion of the curriculum, and how this Learning Situation assesses it:

  • 4.1 · Reading: understand and interpret the overall meaning, structure, key information and purpose of texts, making inferences.
    In this LS: Completes a reading log on two texts about celebrations, noting what, when and where each one takes place, its main customs and two words whose meaning they inferred from context. (O1)
  • 4.2 · Reading: critically assess the content, quality and reliability of texts and the channel they come from.
    In this LS: Rates each source in the research sheet as reliable or not with at least two reasons (author, date, purpose, type of website or channel) and says which one they trust most. (O2)
  • 2.2 · Listening: interpret and value the content and features of spoken texts from everyday life, the media and learning.
    In this LS: After watching two short videos of people describing how they celebrate a festival, explains in a listening chart what each speaker values about it and which words, tone or images show it. (O1)
  • 3.2 · Speaking: plan, produce, review and cooperate to create coherent texts, using suitable physical or digital resources for the audience.
    In this LS: Plans, rehearses and records the 2–3 minute group video pitch with the group, sharing out the parts, and explains their part of the celebration (what, when, where, customs, origin) from a script revised so that an audience that does not know it can follow it. (O3)
  • 5.1 · Writing: write clear, coherent and appropriate texts of medium length, respecting intellectual property and avoiding plagiarism.
    In this LS: Writes an individual formal email to the head teacher (Insecure: guided template) that states the purpose, describes the plan and makes a request, using future forms, relative clauses and the conventions of a formal email. (O5)
  • 1.1 · Intercultural awareness: critically value cultural diversity and adapt to it, building a shared culture.
    In this LS: Explains in the pitch what the celebration means for the people who celebrate it and why it deserves a place at school, with at least one argument about respect for cultural diversity. (O3)
  • 1.2 · Intercultural awareness: take part respectfully in intercultural situations, building links between cultures and rejecting discrimination, prejudice and stereotypes.
    In this LS: Shows in the pitch at least one link between the chosen celebration and a local one (Safe and Daring: similarities and differences) and proposes at least one decision that makes the day inclusive and avoids stereotypes or caricature costumes. (O4)
  • 6.1 · Research: search, select and contrast information in different sources, judging how reliable and relevant they are.
    In this LS: Fills in a research sheet on the chosen celebration with at least two sources (Safe and Daring: three), noting the key information from each source, where they agree or differ, and the full reference. (O2)
  • CC1 1.3 · Citizenship: propose actions that help everyone live together.
    In this LS: Includes in the email to the head teacher at least two concrete actions that help every student and family feel welcome on the celebration day (for example, explaining the customs, inviting families or offering an alternative to those who do not celebrate it). (O5)
  • CC3 3.2 · Citizenship: take an active part in overcoming stereotypes and prejudice.
    In this LS: Names in the pitch at least one stereotype or prejudice about the people who celebrate the festival, explains why it is unfair and shows how the group's plan avoids it (respectful images and words, no caricature costumes). (O4)

Grammar & discourse

  • Present Simple for customs; It is celebrated on… / It takes place in…
  • Prepositions of time: on 31 October, in November, at Christmas
  • Future forms: going to, will, Present Continuous, Present Simple for timetables
  • Origins: Past Simple and used to (Safe, Daring)
  • Relative clauses: which / that, who, where, when (Safe, Daring)
  • Comparing: both… and, whereas, while, however, similar to, different from
  • Formal requests: Could we…? Would it be possible to…?
  • Daring: non-defining relative clauses, Present Simple passive, look forward to + -ing, although / even though

Vocabulary & expressions

  • Seasons, weather and seasonal food: frost, chilly, harvest, chestnuts…
  • Celebrations and customs: festival, tradition, decorations, dress up, light candles…
  • Dates and time: on the night before, once a year, ordinal dates
  • Feelings: excited, grateful, proud…
  • Traditions (Safe): get together, pass down, mark an occasion, exchange gifts
  • Event planning (Safe): set up, run a stall, be held, send invitations
  • Formal email: I am writing to propose… / I look forward to hearing from you
  • Meaning, identity and change (Daring): heritage, commemorate, keep a tradition alive, stereotype, inclusive
