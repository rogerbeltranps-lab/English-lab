2 · He / she / it

-s or no -s?

✋ 1 finger = -s · 2 fingers = no -s

One person, thing or celebration (he / she / it) → verb + -s: Diwali lasts · My grandmother cooks.

Plural (they) → no -s. Careful: people, children and families are plural.

3 · Negatives and questions

don’t, doesn’t or does?

✋ Show 1, 2 or 3 fingers

We to school on 25 December.

don’t go. We → don’t + verb.

your family decorate the house? — Yes, it does.

Does. your family = it → Does…? And the verb has no -s: decorate.

Negative: don’t / doesn’t + verb

We don’t go to school on 25 December.

Question: Do / Does + subject + verb?

Does Diwali last five days? — Yes, it does.

After does / doesn’t, the verb has no -s.

on, in or at?

💬 Turn and talk

What is the rule? When do we use on, in and at?

on

+ date or day

on 31 October · on New Year’s Eve

in

+ month or season

in November · in winter

at

+ festival time, night or clock time

at Christmas · at night · at ten o’clock

Who celebrates it? Does the sentence say?

No. The custom is important, not the people. This is the Present Simple passive.

am / is / are + past participle

To focus on the custom, not the people:

Pumpkins are carved. Gifts are exchanged at midnight.

Negative: isn’t / aren’t + past participle.
