Warm-up · Present Simple

The routine system

I / you / we / they+base verb· I work.
he / she / it+verb-s· She works. He watches TV.
don’t / doesn’t+base verb· She doesn’t study at night.
Do / Does+subject+base verb…?

Short answers: Yes, I do. / No, I don’t. · Yes, she does. / No, she doesn’t.

always · usually · often · sometimes · never go before the main verb, but after be: She usually studies after school. She is often tired.

Amb he / she / it, afegeix -s o -es. Amb doesn’t, el verb principal no porta -s. Never ja vol dir mai: no afegeixis don’t / doesn’t.

2 · Prediction

Literal, partly idiomatic or idiomatic?

Students vote first. Then reveal.

get up

What do you think?

Quite literal. You get out of bed and go up. The meaning is easy to picture.

head out

What do you think?

Partly idiomatic. It means leave / start going somewhere. Optional preposition for + place: I head out. / I head out for school.

turn in

What do you think?

Idiomatic. It means go to bed. You can’t guess it from turn + in.

3 · Before separation

What is an object?

Quick test: ask WHAT? or WHO?

If the sentence gives you an answer, that answer is often the object.

I wake up my brother. → Wake up WHO? → my brother

I catch up on emails. → Catch up on WHAT? → emails

A time like at ten answers WHEN?, so it is not an object.

L’objecte és la persona o cosa afectada per l’acció. Pregunta WHAT? (què?) o WHO? (qui?). at ten respon WHEN? (quan?), no és objecte.

A student points to the object, you click it. No object? Click No object.

3 · Transitive or intransitive?

Is there an object?

Transitive

has an object

wake up my brother
catch up on emails
pick up the kids

Intransitive

has no object

We head out at eight.
We go out on Fridays. I stay in.
I wind down. I turn in.

Two different questions

1. Transitive or intransitive? → Is there an object?

2. Separable or inseparable? → If there is an object, where can it go?

Transitiu = té objecte. Intransitiu = no té objecte. Alguns phrasal verbs poden ser les dues coses: I wake up / I wake him up.

wake up appears twice. So what changes?

I wake up at seven. No object → intransitive.
I wake him up. him is the object → transitive.
The same phrasal verb can be transitive in one sentence and intransitive in another.

5 · Pronoun trap

Now replace the object with it / them

him replaces the object my brother. With a separable phrasal verb, an object pronoun goes in the middle:

verb+pronoun+particle

I wake him up. · I pick them up. · I put it off.

I wake up him. ✗ · I pick up them. ✗

▶ Hear “pick them up” on YouGlish

Amb un phrasal verb separable, el pronom (it, them, him, her) va al mig: pick them up. Amb un nom, les dues posicions són correctes.

6 · Two or three words

Some phrasal verbs have three parts

Two parts
verb+particle

wind down · pick up · put off

Some two-part verbs can add an optional preposition. It is not part of the verb: I head out. / I head out for school. · I wind down. / I wind down with music.

Three parts
verb+particle+preposition

catch up on · get down to

In these verbs the preposition is always needed, and it always needs an object straight after it: catch up on emails, get down to it. So three-part phrasal verbs always have an object, and you can’t split them.

Alguns phrasal verbs tenen tres paraules: verb + partícula + preposició (catch up on, get down to). La preposició necessita l’objecte just després, per això no es poden separar.

Does it need a preposition?

Fill the gap with on, to or nothing. The answer labels every part.

7 · Fixed / inseparable

Keep all the parts together

catch up on and get down to keep all their parts together. The noun or pronoun follows the complete expression.

I catch up on a show. → I catch up on it.

I get down to my homework. → I get down to it.

These three-part expressions have a verb, a particle and a preposition: catch up on, get down to. The preposition needs the object straight after it, so the object can’t go in the middle.

Les expressions fixes no es separen mai: l’objecte (nom o pronom) va al final. I get down to it, no I get it down to.

I catch them up on.

I catch up on them.

✗

I get it down to.

I get down to it.

✗

I catch emails up on.

I catch up on emails.

✗

I get to down my homework.

I get down to my homework.

✗

9 · Compare and explain

Same pronoun, different position. Why?

I wake him up. ✓

wake up is separable and him is a pronoun, so the pronoun must go between the verb and the particle.

I catch up on them. ✓

catch up on is inseparable. The pronoun stays after the complete phrasal verb.

I put it off. ✓

put off is separable, so the pronoun goes in the middle.

I get down to it. ✓

get down to is fixed. The pronoun is the same, but its position follows the expression’s pattern. Context does not make an incorrect position acceptable.

13 · Exit ticket

Can you explain the rule?

Why do we say pick them up
but catch up on them?
Because pick up is separable, so a pronoun goes between the verb and the particle. Catch up on is inseparable, so the pronoun comes after the whole phrasal verb.
